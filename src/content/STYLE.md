# Style for Factura content

Read this before writing or rewriting anything a reader sees: body, title,
`titleTag`, description, summary, CTA, FAQ answers, `<ClosingCta>` copy, alt
text. `AUTHORING.md` decides what a page may say (facts, product claims,
fields, structure); this file decides how it sounds. Where they conflict,
`AUTHORING.md` wins.

Most of the site was drafted by models and it shows, in a small set of habits
a reader learns to recognise within two paragraphs. The test behind every rule
here: does the sentence inform, or does it perform? Performed writing stages a
fact instead of stating it. The fix is always the plain version, never a
synonym for the flagged word.

## Procedure

1. Draft with §1–§4 in mind.
2. Right before you save, re-read §2 and scan the draft for its forms. Rules
   read early in a long session fade; this step exists for that.
3. Fix the passage, not the word. The flagged phrase swapped for its synonym
   inside the same sentence shape is the same problem.
4. One instance of a pattern may stay when it does real work. The same one
   twice, or several different ones in one section, is the failure.
5. Do not overcorrect into clipped bluntness. Staccato one-line closers and a
   page of six-word sentences are tells too. Vary length, keep the connectives.

## 1. State the plain thing

- First sentence of every paragraph, answer and description carries the fact.
  Delete warm-ups ("Hoy en día", "En esta guía", "Comprender esto te ayudará
  a", "Muchas personas se preguntan") and announcements of what the text is
  about to do.
- Name the actor and use the ordinary verb: "ABSA actualiza la tarifa cada
  dos meses", not "se procede a la actualización bimestral". `hacer`, `pagar`,
  `tener`, `usar`, `ser`, not `realizar`, `abonar`, `contar con`, `utilizar`,
  `representar`, `constituir`, `se trata de`.
- A rating word (`clave`, `fundamental`, `esencial`, `crucial`,
  `imprescindible`, `sumamente`, `muchísimo`, `de gran importancia`) is
  replaced by the fact that would make it true. No fact, no claim.
- Attribute or drop: "según el IIEP", "el cuadro tarifario del ENRE", never
  "los expertos", "los datos muestran", "se sabe que". Keep `puede`, `suele`,
  `según` where the evidence needs them.
- The primary keyword sits once in the first sentence or two, in a sentence a
  person would write. Do not re-insert it ("al investigar qué es expensas en
  Argentina, notarás"). Accents and `¿¡` everywhere a reader sees text; the
  unaccented lowercase form belongs only in `keywords` and slugs.
- End at the last useful point. No "En resumen", "En definitiva", "En
  conclusión", no paragraph that repeats the intro.

## 2. Do not stage it

These are the site's own habits, roughly in order of frequency. Each one is a plain
statement dressed as a reveal.

- **Negation then reveal.** "No es X: es Y", "No es X. Es Y.", "no está en X;
  está en Y", "no se trata de X, sino de Y", "no es un lujo sino una
  necesidad". Write Y. The contrast stays only when a reader would plausibly
  believe X and the page needs to correct it: "es un precio de publicación, no
  de escritura" prevents a real mistake; "el engaño no está en la tecnología;
  está en el apuro" prevents nothing.
- **Suspense openers.** "Conviene decirlo con todas las letras:", "Aquí está
  el mecanismo que más confusión genera.", "Esta es la parte que más
  confunde.", "el detalle que hace la diferencia es este:", "La regla es
  simple y no admite excepción:", "Lo cierto es que", "La buena noticia es
  que", "por una razón:". Delete the opener, keep what follows.
- **Advice hedges.** "Conviene", "vale la pena", "es importante", "tené
  presente", "no está de más". The page is the advice; give the instruction:
  "Revisá la categoría antes que nada." One `conviene` per page at most.
- **Counted lead-ins.** "Dos cosas que conviene tener presentes:", "Tres
  consecuencias de esa metodología:", "Los requisitos son tres:". Keep a
  count only when the list is the complete set and follows immediately; drop
  the wrapper ("Cada mes, revisá:").
- **Self-answered questions.** "Muchas personas se preguntan si… La respuesta
  es no." "¿La clave? El consumo." Questions belong in the FAQ and in
  headings a reader would type; prose states the answer.
- **Mic-drop closers.** "Tres juntas alcanzan para no seguir." "Y listo."
  "Así de simple." A one-line sentence dropped after a paragraph for effect
  folds into the sentence before it.
- **Drumbeats.** "Sin filas. Sin papeles. Sin demoras.", three parallel
  openings in a row, three adjectives where one was true, "cambian los rubros
  y cambian los montos".

## 3. Words and marks

- **No raya (`—`).** Legitimate Spanish punctuation and still the most
  frequent single tell on the site, because it arrives as a pivot before the
  point. Commas, parentheses or a new sentence. Semicolons are rare. No
  exclamation marks, no emoji.
- **Bold marks a term the reader scans for**, not emphasis. When writing new
  text, do not bold the first clause of every bullet ("**Cargo fijo.** Se
  paga…" down the whole list). This is a rule for new text; on a rewrite,
  existing bold is kept (§5).
- **Gerund tails and adverb pads.** "…dejando atrás el uso de efectivo",
  "garantizando", "permitiendo así", "de manera eficiente", "de forma fácil y
  segura". Delete the tail or state its checkable consequence.
- **Essay connectors** as sentence openers: "Además", "Asimismo", "Por otro
  lado", "En este sentido", "Cabe destacar", "Es por eso que", "De este
  modo", "A la hora de", "En cuanto a". `Y`, `pero`, `así que`, `porque`
  carry the load.
- **SEO vocabulary.** "Descubrí", "todo lo que necesitás saber", "guía
  completa", "paso a paso" as filler, "gestionar", "optimizar", "brindar",
  "garantizar", "eficiente", "integral", "herramienta". Say what the thing
  does.
- **Promises.** "En segundos", "sin errores", "con total seguridad",
  "correctamente" about the product are claims §6 of `AUTHORING.md` forbids;
  about a trámite they need the source.

## 4. Voseo, consistently

Argentine Spanish, `vos`, the register of a good accountant explaining
something (`AUTHORING.md` §5 has the vocabulary rules). The slip that actually
happens is a `tú` verb inside a `vos` page, usually an imperative. Check every
imperative and every second-person present:

| tú (wrong here)                             | vos                                       |
| ------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------- |
| puedes, tienes, quieres, sabes, eres, debes | podés, tenés, querés, sabés, sos, debés   |
| mira, revisa, anota, paga, compara, busca   | mirá, revisá, anotá, pagá, compará, buscá |
| fíjate, búscalo, anótalo, súbela            | fijate, buscalo, anotalo, subila          |
| ten, ven, haz, pon, di, sal                 | tené, vení, hacé, poné, decí, salí        |
| sube, elige, divide, pide, empieza          | subí, elegí, dividí, pedí, empezá         |
| tú, ti, contigo; vosotros                   | vos, vos, con vos; ustedes                |

Voseo is the address, not a licence for slang, exclamation or chumminess.

## 5. Rewriting an existing page

- Change sentences, not the page. Passages this file flags get rewritten;
  sentences that already read plainly stay word for word.
- Preserve every fact, figure, date, unit, name, link and its target, every
  component tag and its position, the FAQ questions as searched, and the
  keyword in title, description and first sentence.
- Keep `##` heading text unless the brief asks otherwise: anchor ids are
  slugged from it and internal links and the contents column point at them.
- Keep the formatting. A numbered list stays a numbered list, a table stays a
  table, and a term the original set in bold stays in bold in the rewritten
  sentence: that bold is part of the page's SEO, and whether the original
  author chose it well is not the rewrite's question. Change the shape of a
  block only when the brief asks, or when the block itself is a flagged
  pattern (a drumbeat of fragments, say) and prose is the fix; then say so
  in your reply. Bold may be added to a term that clearly belongs with the
  ones already bolded, never taken away.
- Length equal or shorter. A rewrite that adds a section, a summary or a
  "qué vas a encontrar" list has widened the brief.
- The product paragraph and `<ClosingCta>` are about this document: what the
  app extracts from a Camuzzi bill, why two comprobantes per bimestre matter.
  The stock sentences ("arma la serie mes a mes", "en un solo tablero", "de un
  vistazo", "la parte tediosa") are already on most other pages.

## 7. Final read

Read the result once as a sceptical reader who has seen the site's habits.
For each section:

1. Does the first sentence carry information?
2. Is anything staged: a negation before the point, an opener promising a
   reveal, a count, a question you answer yourself, a one-line closer?
3. Can any adjective, connector, gerund tail or recap go without loss?
4. Is every imperative and second-person verb a `vos` form?
5. Does every number, certainty and product claim have its support on the
   page?
